**Work in Progress**

Early in my career, I worked as a Training Specialist and Instructional Designer for a company that produced excellent instructional manuals grounded in consistency. I learned from the best.

**Each process was broken into three phases:**

1. Summary paragraph. Every process opened with a short paragraph summarizing and explaining the feature or functionality: what it is, why it matters, and when the user would need it. This gave users context before they touched anything, so the steps that followed had meaning rather than feeling like a list of clicks to memorize.

2. Read-through instructions. Next came a narrative walkthrough of the process from start to finish. Some people learn best by reading through a process before doing it, and this phase was designed for them. Users could read it before attempting the task to understand the overall flow, what to expect along the way, and how each part connected to the next. It built a mental map, so nothing in the steps came as a surprise.

3. Step-by-step instructions. Finally, users got clear, numbered steps to follow while performing the task. Each step contained a single action, written in plain, concise language that was easy to follow. There were no crowded paragraphs or steps packed with multiple instructions, just one clear action at a time, paired with a screenshot showing exactly what the user should see on screen. Shortcuts and tips were called out along the way, giving new users helpful context and experienced users faster ways to work. Everything was written consistently from one manual to the next, so users always knew where to look and what to do.

The result was a structure that worked for every kind of learner. Those who needed the "why" got it up front, those who wanted the big picture got the read-through, and those who just needed to get the task done could jump straight to the steps. And because every manual followed the same format, users learned how to learn from them, which made each new process easier to pick up than the last.


Here is a very basic example


**Summary Paragraph**


Application Summary displays the summary of the application including the type of mortgage and terms of loan, property information and purpose of loan, and borrower information. 


**Read-through instructions**


Select the transaction you wish to view from the Files Pipeline view.  Select **View Application Summary** from the Action Menu.  Click the **Pipeline** tab to return to the Pipeline.


**Step by Step Insructions**


1. Select the transaction you wish to view from the Files Pipeline view.
   *Click the applicants name to view applicant details and contact information.*
2. Select **View Application Summary** from the Action Menu.  
3. Click the **Pipeline** tab to return to the Pipeline.

---
I often don't see or don't use the Read-Through instructions, but I do always use the Summary Paragraph and the Step by Step Instructions.
